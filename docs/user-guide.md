# LearnGrid User Guide

LearnGrid is your school’s own learning space. Teachers publish lessons and mark work. Students open those lessons and hand in assignments. Parents check homework and grades for their own children. School administrators set up the year, the classes, and the people.

Every screen uses your school’s name, logo, and colours. The same steps work on a phone and on a computer. Phones are the main way students and parents will use it.

This guide is for the people who will train the rest of the school. Start with Part 1 if you are setting the school up. Teachers, students, and parents can go straight to their own part.

A good order for the first training day:

1. Sign in and look around the Dashboard.
2. Set the colours and logo.
3. Create the year, a term, the classes, and the subjects.
4. Give teachers and students their logins, then put them in the right classes.
5. Link each parent to their child.
6. Have one teacher publish a short lesson and one assignment, then have one student hand it in.

---

## Part 1: School Administrator Onboarding (TOT)

You are the person who prepares the school before anyone else starts teaching. After you sign in, the left-hand menu shows **Dashboard**, **Setup**, **People**, **Academic**, **Content**, and **Settings**. On a phone, open that menu from the side.

Your own name and sign-in email live under **Settings**. The school’s look lives under **Setup**.

### How to set up the school profile and branding

The school name is already in place when your LearnGrid address is opened for the pilot. You choose the logo and the two colours everyone will see.

1. Sign in with the email and password you were given.
2. Open **Setup**. The page title is **Branding Settings**.
3. Under **School Logo**, choose a JPEG, PNG, or WebP picture. Keep it under 10 MB. This picture is used when you have not set a separate header logo.
4. Under **Header Logo**, add a wide picture if you want one at the top of the school’s pages. The same file types and size limit apply.
5. Set **Primary color** and **Secondary color**. A preview on the right shows how a student page will look. Change the colours until that preview feels like your school.
6. Select **Save Branding**. Wait until the button is ready again before you leave the page.
7. If you want to start over, select **Reset to defaults**, then set the colours again and save.

Open the school on a phone after you save. Check that the logo is readable and the buttons are easy to see in daylight.

### How to create Academic Years, Terms, Classes, and Subjects

Do this before you assign teachers or students. LearnGrid needs one current year, then terms inside that year, then classes and subjects.

1. Open **Academic**. The overview lists the setup in order: Academic Years, Terms, Classes, Subjects.
2. Open **Years**, or select **Add academic year** on the overview.
3. Enter a **Name** people will recognise, such as `2026–2027`.
4. Enter the **Start date** and **End date**.
5. Tick **Mark as current**. Only the current year is used when you place students in classes.
6. Select **Save**.
7. Open **Terms**. You should see the current year’s name at the top. If you see “Set a current academic year,” go back and mark a year as current.
8. Select **Add term**. Choose the academic year, enter a name such as `Term 1`, and set the start and end dates inside that year.
9. Tick **Mark as current** for the term you are teaching now, then select **Save**. You can add later terms the same way. Use **Mark current** on the list when the school moves to the next term.
10. Open **Classes** and select **Add class**. Enter the class name the way staff already say it, such as `Grade 5` or `JSS 1`. Select **Save**. Repeat for each class in the pilot.
11. Open **Subjects** and select **Add subject**. Enter the subject name, such as `Mathematics`. Select **Save**.
12. Still on **Subjects**, select **Link to class**. Choose the subject and the class, then save. Do this for every subject a class will study. A teacher cannot be assigned until the subject is linked to the class.

You can return to any of these lists later to edit a name or add another class.

### How to invite Teachers, Students, and link Parents

There are two different tools, and they do different jobs.

**Invite** only records an email on the Pending list. LearnGrid does not send a message, and that person cannot sign in yet.

**Bulk Import CSV** creates real logins for teachers and students. Each new person gets a temporary password. That password is shown once, in the report on your screen. Write it down or copy it before you close the window, then give it to the person in a way your school trusts.

#### Teachers and students, one at a time (pending only)

Use this when you only need a reminder of who still needs an account.

1. Open **People**, then **Teachers**, **Students**, or **Admins**.
2. Type the person’s email.
3. Select **Invite teacher**, **Invite Student**, or **Invite School admin**.
4. You should see “Invitation saved. No email was sent.” The email appears under **Pending invitations**.

This step does not create a password. Use the import below when the person needs to sign in during the pilot.

#### Teachers and students, as a group (creates logins)

Prepare the classes and subject links first. The file must use class names and subject names that already exist.

1. Open **People**, then **Teachers** or **Students**.
2. Select **Bulk Import CSV**.
3. Select **Download sample CSV** and open it in a spreadsheet.
4. For students, fill in first name, last name, email, and class name. One student per row.
5. For teachers, fill in first name, last name, email, class name, and subject name. The class and subject must already be linked.
6. Save the file. You can import up to 100 rows at a time.
7. Choose the file and start the import.
8. Read the report. Copy every temporary password before you close it. Hand each person their email and temporary password, and ask them to sign in and choose a new password the same day.

If a row fails, the report says why. Fix that row and import it again. A common reason is a class name that does not match the class you created, including extra spaces.

#### Parents

A parent can only be linked after they already have a LearnGrid login and appear in the Parents list. The parent invite, like the others, saves a pending email and does not send a message or create a password. If a parent is missing from the list, ask the LearnGrid person who opened your pilot to create that login. Then:

1. Open **People**, then **Parents**.
2. Select the parent’s name. Their email is under the name.
3. In **Link a child**, search for the student and choose them.
4. Confirm the link. The child’s name appears under **Linked children**.
5. Repeat for each child. One parent can be linked to more than one student.

A parent with no linked child will see “No children linked to your account” and will be told to contact you.

### How to assign Teachers to classes

A teacher only sees classes you assign. The choice is always a class and a subject together, such as `Grade 5 · Mathematics`.

1. Finish **Link to class** on the Subjects page for every class and subject in the pilot.
2. Open **People**, then **Teachers**.
3. Find the teacher under **Active teachers**.
4. Select **Assign class**.
5. In **Class and subject**, choose the right pair.
6. Select **Save**.

The class and subject appear in that teacher’s row. Repeat for each subject they teach. If the button tells you to link a subject first, go back to **Academic**, then **Subjects**, and use **Link to class**.

Students are placed in a class the same way: open **People**, then **Students**, and select **Assign class**. You need a current academic year and at least one class. Otherwise the button explains what is still missing.

After this, ask the teacher to sign in. Their Home page should list the class you assigned.

## Part 2: Teacher Quick-Start

Your menu is **Home**, **Classes**, **Lessons**, **Assignments**, **Grading**, and **Settings**. Home is the place to start each day. It shows your classes, recent lessons, and work that still needs a grade.

If Home says you have no classes, ask a school administrator to assign you. You cannot create a lesson until that assignment exists.

### How to log in and view your classes

1. Open the school’s LearnGrid address on your phone or computer.
2. Enter your email and password, then select **Sign in**. If the button looks faded, wait a moment for the page to finish loading.
3. You land on **Teacher Home**. Your name is at the top. Your classes are listed underneath.
4. Open **Classes** in the menu to see the students, subjects, and courses for each class you teach.

The first time you sign in with a temporary password, change it the same day. On the sign-in page, use **Forgot password**, or ask your school administrator if the reset email does not arrive.

### How to create and publish a Lesson (including how to add PDFs or YouTube links)

Students only see a lesson after you publish it. A draft is visible to you, not to the class.

1. From Home or **Lessons**, select **Create Lesson**.
2. Choose the **Class**, **Subject**, and **Course**. You only see classes you have been assigned.
3. Enter a **Title** students will recognise, such as `Fractions: Introduction`.
4. Write the lesson in **Description**. Short paragraphs are easier to read on a phone.
5. Select **Save Draft**. You return to the lesson list, and the lesson is marked **DRAFT**.
6. Open that lesson by its title.
7. Under **Materials**, add files or links.
   - To add a PDF, Word document, or a JPEG, PNG, or WebP image, choose the file and select **Add file**. Keep each file under 10 MB so students on slow connections can open it.
   - To add a YouTube video, or any other web link, paste the full address (it starts with `https://`) and select **Add link**. Students will see a **Watch** button that opens the video in a new tab. The video itself plays on YouTube, so it uses more data than a PDF.
8. When the title, description, and materials are ready, select **Publish Lesson**.

You can keep editing a published lesson. **Save** keeps your edits. **Publish Lesson** makes the latest version visible to students.

To reuse a lesson in another class, open the lesson and select **Duplicate**. Choose the other course and select **Create draft**. The copy starts as a draft, so you can change it before you publish.

### How to create an Assignment or a Quiz

An assignment is work the student hands in on LearnGrid. A quiz, test, or exam is work you mark on paper, then enter the scores yourself. Students do not upload a file for a quiz.

1. Open the published lesson.
2. Select **Create Assessment**.
3. Enter a **Title**, such as `Assignment: Fractions practice` or `Paper quiz: Mathematics`.
4. Set **Type** to **Assignment**, **Quiz**, **Test**, or **Exam**.
5. Set a **Due date** if the work has one.
6. Enter a **Max score**. This is required for a quiz, test, or exam. It is optional for an assignment, but students understand a mark more easily when you set one, such as `20`.
7. Select **Publish** when the class should see it. **Save as Draft** keeps it hidden from students.

Published assignments show on the student Home page under **To hand in**.

### How to grade submissions and release scores

Students cannot see a score until you release it. Saving a mark is not the same as releasing it.

#### Assignments students handed in

1. Open **Grading**, or open the assignment from Home.
2. Select the assignment title.
3. Select the student. Read **Your answer** and select **View File** if they attached one.
4. Enter the **Grade**. The number next to the box is the maximum, such as `/ 20`.
5. Write a short note in **Feedback**. Say what was done well and what to try next.
6. Select **Release Grade**.

After you release it, the student and their linked parent can see the score and the feedback. Until then, it stays with you.

#### Quizzes, tests, and exams marked on paper

1. Open **Grading** and select the quiz, test, or exam.
2. You see **Grade entry**: one row for each student in the class. There is no file to open.
3. Type each **Score** and a short **Feedback** note.
4. Select **Save Grades** if you want to finish later without showing marks.
5. Select **Release All** when the marks are ready for students and parents.

Check the list for the word **Released** before you tell the class that grades are available.

## Part 3: Student Guide

You only need your school’s web address, your email, and your password. A teacher or the school office will give these to you.

### How to log in on your phone

1. Open the phone’s browser. Chrome is a good choice. You do not need to install an app.
2. Type the school’s LearnGrid address. It is the link on the paper or message from your school, not a general search result.
3. Enter your **Email** and **Password**.
4. Tap **Sign in**. If the button looks grey, wait until the page has finished loading, then tap it once.
5. You should see your name and the words **Student Home**.

Along the bottom of the phone you will see **Home**, **Classes**, **Grades**, and **Settings**. Home is your learning list for today.

The first time you use a temporary password, ask a teacher to help you reset it. See Part 5.

### How to find lessons and submit assignments

1. On **Home**, look at **To hand in** first. Those are assignments with a due date.
2. Under **Lessons**, tap **Open Lesson** on the one your teacher named in class.
3. Read the description. Under **Materials**, tap **Download** for a PDF or picture, or **Watch** for a video link. A video opens in a new tab.
4. Tap **Back to Learning Feed** when you are finished.
5. For an assignment, tap **Open Assignment**.
6. Read the due date.
7. Type your work in **Your answer**. If your teacher asked for a file, add a PDF, Word document, or a JPEG, PNG, or WebP picture. The file must be 10 MB or smaller.
8. Tap **Hand in**. Wait until the page finishes. Do not close the browser while it says **Handing in...**.
9. If you already handed in and need to add a file, use **Attach file**.

**Classes** shows the classes you belong to. If Home says you are not enrolled in any classes, tell your teacher. A school administrator still needs to place you in a class.

Quizzes and paper tests do not have a Hand in button. Your teacher enters those marks after the paper is marked.

### How to check your grades

1. Tap **Grades**.
2. Marks are grouped by class and subject.
3. Each card shows the title, the score, and the teacher’s feedback when they wrote some.

You only see a grade after the teacher releases it. If you handed work in and nothing is listed yet, the teacher may still be marking. An unreleased paper quiz will not appear here.

### What to do if your internet is slow

LearnGrid is built to stay simple on a phone, but it still needs a connection while you sign in, open a lesson, or hand work in. It does not store lessons on the phone for later.

1. Stay on the school’s Wi‑Fi when you can. If you are on mobile data, do the writing first and save downloads for later.
2. Wait for the page to finish loading before you tap **Sign in**, **Save**, or **Hand in**. Tapping early does nothing.
3. Read the lesson text on the page. It uses less data than a video.
4. Download a PDF once, when the signal is steady, and read that file. Avoid downloading the same file again and again.
5. Tap **Watch** only when you have enough data. A YouTube link plays outside LearnGrid.
6. Type your answer in the box when you can. A long written answer is much smaller than a photo of the page.
7. If you must attach a photo, take it in good light and keep it small. The limit is 10 MB.
8. If a page fails, wait a minute and try the same step again. Do not start a second hand-in until you know whether the first one went through. Ask your teacher if you are unsure.

## Part 4: Parent Guide

You can see homework and grades for the children the school has linked to you. You cannot change lessons, submit work, or see other families.

### How to log in

1. Open the school’s LearnGrid address in your phone’s browser.
2. Enter the email and password the school gave you.
3. Tap **Sign in**.
4. You should see **Parent Home** and your name.

The bottom of the screen has **Home**, **Assignments**, **Grades**, and **Settings**.

If you see “No children linked to your account,” contact the school administrator. They link your child. You cannot add a child yourself.

### How to switch between multiple children

When more than one child is linked to you, their names appear under **Children** on Home, Assignments, and Grades.

1. Tap the child’s name.
2. The page reloads for that child. Upcoming work, overdue work, and grades all change to match the child you tapped.
3. Open **Assignments** or **Grades** after you switch. The choice stays with you while you move between those pages.

If you have only one child, you will see that child’s name and no extra buttons.

### How to check upcoming homework and recent grades

On **Home** you will see three lists:

1. **Upcoming** shows work that is still due. Open **Assignments** in the menu for the fuller list.
2. **Overdue** shows work that is past the due date and still needs attention.
3. **Recent grades** shows marks the teacher has released, including the score and any feedback.

A quiet list is normal. “No upcoming assignments” means nothing is due right now. “No released grades yet” means the teacher has not released marks, not that your child has no work.

You will not see a grade the teacher has not released. Paper quizzes appear only after the teacher releases them.

## Part 5: Troubleshooting

### What to do if you forget your password

1. Open the sign-in page.
2. Tap **Forgot password**.
3. Enter the same email you use to sign in, then tap **Send reset link**.
4. Check your email, including the spam or junk folder. Open the reset link on the same phone or computer if you can.
5. Choose a new password on the page that opens, then return to sign in.

If no email arrives:

- Wait ten minutes and request the link once more. Several requests in a row can slow the mailbox down.
- Confirm with the school office that they have the same email address you typed.
- Ask the school administrator to read your temporary password again if your account was created from a class list and you have never signed in. They can only see that password at the moment the account is created, so they may need to set you up again.

Do not share your password in a class group chat. Ask the office to give it to you directly.

### Who to contact if your account is locked or your school's access is suspended

**One person cannot sign in.** Contact your school administrator. Tell them the email you typed and what the page said after **Sign in**. They can check that you are on the active list, that you are in a class, and that a parent is linked to the right child. A faded **Sign in** button usually means the page is still loading. Wait, then try once.

**A teacher can sign in but sees no classes.** The administrator still needs to assign that teacher to a class and subject. See Part 1.

**A student can sign in but sees no lessons.** The teacher may not have published the lesson yet, or the student may not be in the class. The student should tell the teacher. The teacher should confirm the lesson says published, not draft.

**Everyone in the school is refused at the same time**, including the school administrator. The school’s LearnGrid access may have been paused or suspended. Teachers, students, and parents should stop retrying and tell the school administrator. The school administrator should contact the LearnGrid person who set up the pilot. Have the school name and the web address ready. Another teacher cannot turn access back on, and resetting a password will not fix a paused school.

**A parent sees the wrong child, or no child.** Contact the school administrator and ask them to check **People**, then **Parents**. They can link or correct the child. Parents cannot change that link themselves.
